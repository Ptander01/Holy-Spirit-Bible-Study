Rooted and Established
"Therefore, as you received Christ Jesus the Lord, so walk in him, rooted and built up in him and established in the faith, just as you were taught, abounding in thanksgiving."
Colossians 2:6–7 · ESV
Session 1 · Ephesians 3:14–21
Rooted, Not Just Planted
Focus: Personal Devotion, the Apostolic Pattern, and a Willing Heart

This series takes its name from two prayers written decades apart, both reaching for the same image. Paul prays that the Ephesians would be "rooted and grounded in love." He tells the Colossians to walk in Christ, "rooted and built up... and established in the faith." Both pictures assume the same thing: real faith isn't planted once and then left alone. It has to actually take root.

A Season of Accountability

Consider this an invitation into a season of honest personal accountability. Not guilt — accountability. How is your intrinsic motivation right now, genuinely? Not what you'd say out loud in a group, but what's actually true when no one's checking.

"That according to the riches of his glory he may grant you to be strengthened with power through his Spirit in your inner being, so that Christ may dwell in your hearts through faith—that you, being rooted and grounded in love, may have strength to comprehend with all the saints what is the breadth and length and height and depth, and to know the love of Christ that surpasses knowledge, that you may be filled with all the fullness of God."
Ephesians 3:16–19
Where the Series Gets Its Name

"Rooted and grounded in love" (Ephesians 3:17) and "rooted... and established in the faith" (Colossians 2:7) are the same image from two different letters. Paul keeps reaching for it because it's the right one: a root system you can't see, doing the work that actually holds everything up.

When God Becomes a Service Provider

There's a quiet drift a lot of us are prone to: treating God like a service provider and church like a marketplace — a place you show up to consume something, evaluate the product, and leave a review. Colossians 2:6–7, above, pictures something else entirely: not a customer, but someone with roots actually growing down into the ground.

"He is like a tree planted by streams of water that yields its fruit in its season, and its leaf does not wither. In all that he does, he prospers."
Psalm 1:3

Psalm 1 pictures the same thing: a tree planted by streams of water, deeply rooted, bearing fruit in its season. Consumers don't put down roots. They just keep moving to wherever the product looks better.

What the First Church Actually Looked Like

"And they devoted themselves to the apostles' teaching and the fellowship, to the breaking of bread and the prayers."
Acts 2:42

Look at what held the earliest church together: unity in the apostles' teaching, and a real relationship with God worked out through actual devotion — not a single decision, but a devoted, ongoing rhythm.

A Bridge Across Time

"That which was from the beginning, which we have heard, which we have seen with our eyes, which we looked upon and have touched with our hands, concerning the word of life—the life was made manifest, and we have seen it, and testify to it and proclaim to you the eternal life, which was with the Father and was made manifest to us—that which we have seen and heard we proclaim also to you, so that you too may have fellowship with us; and indeed our fellowship is with the Father and with his Son Jesus Christ. And we are writing these things so that our joy may be complete."
1 John 1:1–4

1 John opens as a powerful bridge back to the original apostles — the ones who were actually present, physically there, together, devoted to the message that carried people out of the old life and into life in Christ. My sin put Jesus on the cross, and I needed to be saved — that was their message, and it still is. And notice how they responded: faith, repentance, and baptism, met with obedience and urgency. But it didn't stop there. They stayed devoted afterward. It wasn't a single emotional event, one-and-done.

How Do We Learn From the Apostles Today?

We don't get to sit at their feet the way the earliest believers did. But we have real access to what they left behind: we can read it ourselves, hear it read aloud and discussed, and hear it preached. The real question isn't access. It's whether you're actually putting in the work to grow and feed on the word, or letting the access sit unused.

Staying Devoted When Life Gets Busy

Review your actual priorities, not the ones you'd claim if asked. Are you really investing the way you say you want to? What are you going to do about the gap?

"But I have this against you, that you have abandoned the love you had at first. Remember therefore from where you have fallen; repent, and do the works you did at first."
Revelation 2:4–5
Worth Sitting With

Action springs from love. Not "I have to" — "I want this. I'm excited about this." If the doing has gone dry, the question underneath is almost always whether the love went quiet first.

Doers, Not Just Hearers

"But be doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving yourselves."
James 1:22

Am I devoted? Do I actually have a willing heart? These aren't just feelings to assess in the abstract — they're measurable, observable in the actual shape of a life.

"And you, Solomon my son, know the God of your father and serve him with a whole heart and with a willing mind, for the Lord searches all hearts and understands every plan and thought. If you seek him, he will be found by you, but if you forsake him, he will cast you off forever."
1 Chronicles 28:9

David's charge to Solomon doesn't ask for perfection — it asks for a whole heart and a willing mind, because God isn't fooled by performance. He searches the heart and understands the thought behind it.

"I know, my God, that you test the heart and have pleasure in uprightness. In the uprightness of my heart I have offered all these things willingly, and now I have seen your people, who are present here, offering freely and joyously to you."
1 Chronicles 29:17

David's own prayer echoes the same thing: God tests the heart, and takes pleasure not in the size of the offering but in the willingness behind it.

What Change Will You Commit To?

Every thread in this session points to the same closing question — not as an idea to admire, but as something to actually act on.

How is your intrinsic motivation right now, honestly — not what you'd say in a group, but what's true when no one's checking?
Where in your life does "consumer Christianity" show up — treating God as a service provider or church as a marketplace you evaluate rather than belong to?
The apostles stayed devoted after their initial response of faith, repentance, and baptism. Where has your own devotion been more of a one-time event than an ongoing rhythm?
What specific change will you commit to that will actually lead toward spiritual maturity — not a vague intention, but something you could point to a month from now?
