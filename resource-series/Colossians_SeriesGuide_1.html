The Letter to the Colossians
"So as to walk in a manner worthy of the Lord, fully pleasing to him: bearing fruit in every good work and increasing in the knowledge of God."
Colossians 1:10 · ESV
Session 1 · Colossians 1:1–14
A Story You Belong To
Focus: The Family of Faith, the Cost of Sin, and a Gospel That Keeps Growing

Every family has a story — the ones you tell around a table, worn smooth from being told so many times. Paul opens this letter reminding a church he had never even visited that they belong to a much bigger story than they realized. The faithful people scattered through Scripture — the wandering Israelites, the exiled remnant, the first believers who staked everything on a crucified rabbi — aren't distant characters in an old book. They're family.

A Story You Can Trace

Think about your own hometown — the streets, the people, the years that actually happened to you. None of that is abstract; it's personal, because you were there, or your people were. Scripture works the same way for anyone who belongs to Christ. You should be able to tell the story of the Bible the way a grandfather tells a story about his own past — not as trivia, but as your history. Enslavement, exile, unfaithfulness, betrayal, sin, victory, rejoicing — these things happened to your people. Your family.

Worth Naming

Don't settle for a superficial spirituality that isn't actually moved by any of this. If Israel's slavery in Egypt or the early church's persecution reads to you like someone else's ancient history, something's missing. This is your family's story.

Always Thanking God for You

"We always thank God, the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, when we pray for you, since we heard of your faith in Christ Jesus and of the love that you have for all the saints,"
Colossians 1:3–4

Paul had never set foot in Colossae. He'd never met most of these people face-to-face. And yet the very first thing out of his pen isn't correction — it's gratitude, prayed on their behalf, every single time he thinks of them.

A Gospel That Won't Stop Growing

"Of this you have heard before in the word of the truth, the gospel, which has come to you, as indeed in the whole world it is bearing fruit and growing—as it also does among you, since the day you heard it and understood the grace of God in truth,"
Colossians 1:5–6

The gospel isn't a message that arrives, gets acknowledged, and sits still. Paul describes it as something alive — bearing fruit and increasing, all over the world, and specifically in them, from the very day they first heard it and actually understood what grace meant.

Remember What Death Felt Like

Sin produces death — isolation, hurt, desolation. Lying. Cheating. Stealing. Selfishness. Lust. Greed. Pride. Anger. Hatred. Comparison. Competition. Even spiritual comparison can be quietly deadly — the kind we just chalk up as "drama" and chuckle at instead of naming for what it actually is.

Worth Sitting With

Do you remember what it was like to actually live in death? Do you miss it? Probably not — and yet even knowing that, there's still a pull to drift back toward it. Or just dabble.

Two Pictures of the Same Growth

  • Luke 8 — The SowerGod's word gets scattered. Some is devoured by the enemy before it can take root; some shoots up fast and dies just as fast the moment life gets hard; some gets choked out by the cares, riches, and pleasures of life. The good soil is a soft, honest heart that holds on and bears fruit with patience.
  • Luke 13 — The Mustard SeedA seed that starts as the smallest thing you've planted and quietly becomes a massive tree. Small beginnings, real growth, given time.

Love in the Spirit

"Just as you learned it from Epaphras our beloved fellow servant. He is a faithful minister of Christ on your behalf and has made known to us your love in the Spirit."
Colossians 1:7–8

It's easy to love people who are basically just like you. Love in the Spirit is different — it's loving people who aren't like you. Left to ourselves, we naturally look down on other people and other groups, not so differently from high schoolers frantically working out the social food chain, tying their whole sense of worth to where they land on it. Meanwhile the gospel says something else entirely: every time I think of you, I thank God for you.

Knowledge Isn't the Same as Wisdom

"And so, from the day we heard, we have not ceased to pray for you, asking that you may be filled with the knowledge of his will in all spiritual wisdom and understanding,"
Colossians 1:9

Knowledge is information. Wisdom is knowing what to do with it. Paul doesn't just pray that they'd know more — he prays they'd be filled with the wisdom to actually act on what they know.

Joining the Rescue

The Real Invitation

God isn't asking you to be a good person, or simply to not be a bad one. He's asking you to join him in his rescue work — to be a powerful, unapologetic light, not a quiet, cautious one.

Who Am I Becoming?

Everything in this passage points to one honest question, worth carrying with you this week.

Can you tell the story of the Bible the way a grandfather tells a family story — like it actually happened to your people? Where does it still feel like someone else's history instead?
Where do you catch yourself dabbling with or drifting back toward the things that once produced death, isolation, and hurt in your life?
Love in the Spirit means loving people who aren't like you. Who is that hardest for you right now — and what would it look like to actually thank God for them?
Who am I becoming? More loving? More joyful? More peaceful? More patient? More kind? Where's the evidence, and where's the gap?
Memory Verse · Session 1
"So as to walk in a manner worthy of the Lord, fully pleasing to him: bearing fruit in every good work and increasing in the knowledge of God."
Colossians 1:10
Ask God to grow in you the same combination Paul prayed for — not just information about him, but the wisdom to actually walk it out.
